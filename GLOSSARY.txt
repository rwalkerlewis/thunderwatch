THUNDERWATCH REPORT FIELD GUIDE
================================

Explains every field and abbreviation in the two report formats: the full
trip report (trip_report.py) and the compact inReach report
(inreach_report.py). Times are 24-hour, no colons (0530 = 5:30 AM local).
A "+1" suffix means the following calendar day (0044+1 = 00:44 next day).
Distances in miles, elevations in feet unless --units km is used.

WHAT P MEANS
------------
P is the forecast probability of lightning occurring within about 15 km
(9 mi) of the route during that clock hour. 15 km is strike range: a flash
that close can hit you regardless of where the storm center is. P comes
from one of two sources, whichever is reachable:
  - Model environment (Open-Meteo blend of HRRR/GFS/ICON): hourly CAPE
    (storm fuel), CIN (the cap suppressing storms), and precipitation
    probability, converted to P by a terrain-tuned mapping. This mapping
    is deliberately aggressive at low CAPE because high mountains trigger
    storms in conditions that stay quiet over flat ground.
  - NWS gridpoint "probability of thunder": the Weather Service's own
    calibrated hourly thunder probability for the grid cell.
P applies to the whole route corridor for that hour. It does NOT say where
along the route the storm would be, and it is NOT conditioned on the
hiker's location. Rough reading: 0.00-0.05 quiet, ~0.1 a stray weak cell
is possible, 0.2-0.4 storms likely somewhere nearby, 0.5+ expect them.
These numbers are not yet statistically calibrated; trust the shape (which
hours, which days) more than the exact values.

WHAT H (HAZARD) MEANS
---------------------
H = P for the hour x terrain exposure at the hiker's PROJECTED position at
that time. This is the number that follows the hiker, not the weather.
Exposure: 1.0 above treeline, sliding down to 0.35 in forest (trees reduce
but do not remove lightning risk), with a bonus on passes and crests
(local high points, nowhere to retreat). So the same P=0.4 hour reads
H=0.40 on a pass but H=0.14 in deep timber.
Bands: LOW < 0.15, MODERATE 0.15-0.35, HIGH 0.35-0.60, EXTREME >= 0.60.
"pk haz" / "peak hazard" is the worst H moment of the whole day and when
it occurs.

TIMELINE
--------
Times come from a hiking-speed model (Tobler's function: speed depends on
slope) scaled by the pace factor. Pace factor 0.7 = typical loaded
backpacker; the factor in use is calibrated from the hiker's own observed
splits, so timeline times are projections at demonstrated pace, breaks
included. Landmarks:
  start        departure point
  Ps1, Ps2...  passes/crests, auto-detected local high points, with
               elevation and the P for the hour of crossing (P0 = 0.00)
  pk haz       moment of peak hazard H (see above)
  sunset       computed for the route location, accurate to a few minutes;
               includes the trail mile the hiker is projected to be at
  end          end of route: distance and elevation
In the full report each line also carries elevation (ft) and trail mile.

LEAD SENTENCE (INREACH FORMAT)
------------------------------
The first sentence summarizes the hiker-following hazard H by hour:
  "Hazard to you zero all hours."
  "Hazard to you zero except 1600 hour at 0.08."
  "Hazard to you: 0900-1359 at 0.35, 1800 hour at 0.42."
Hours are local. Timeline entries carry H at each point (H0 = zero);
pass lines in the full report show both H and the raw corridor P.
The full report's hourly table has a P row (corridor) and an H row
(follows the hiker); they differ when the hiker is below treeline or
in camp during elevated hours. If the day runs far off schedule, the
H row inherits the error - fall back to the P row.

OTHER ABBREVIATIONS
-------------------
iss       issued (when the forecast was generated) - forecasts go stale;
          a report issued the evening before is good, one from two days
          ago is background only
mi8.6     trail mile 8.6
H0        hazard zero at that point and hour
(+1)      next calendar day

FULL-REPORT-ONLY FIELDS
-----------------------
ROUTE line     total distance, total climb, start/high/end elevations
HAZARD line    peak H, band, time; whether passes cross at P=0
CURRENT line   (when present) live conditions at issue time: number of
               radar-tracked storm cells within 155 mi and satellite-
               detected lightning flashes within 62 mi in the last 10 min.
               Zeros here mean the region is quiet right now, which says
               nothing about the afternoon.
BOTTOM LINE    the one-sentence operational summary

LIMITS TO KEEP IN MIND
----------------------
- Forecast skill decays with lead time. Same-morning reports are the real
  product; 2-3 day reports show the pattern, not the hours.
- P is one number for the whole corridor from one model grid cell. It
  cannot say which basin or which pass a storm will pick.
- The timeline is a pace projection. If actual travel runs ahead or behind,
  every downstream time shifts with it, including which hours the exposed
  crossings land in.
- None of this replaces looking at the sky. Building cumulus by late
  morning over the crests is information no report supersedes.
